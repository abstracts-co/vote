# American politics map

Who is in office, who is taking office, who is running, what they stand for and how far left or right they lean. Updated 2026-11-06. Election Day is Nov 7, 2028.

## Who holds power

Senate: 47-53 now, 54-44 in January (Democrats first, unofficial). House: 214-218 now, 250-162 in January. Governors: 24-26 now, 28-20 in January.

## President

### Andy Beshear

Governor of Kentucky. Considering.

Beshear says he is considering a run because he does not want his children to inherit a divided, damaged country. He says the test is whether he can repair that damage and bring people together; otherwise, he would support someone who can.

- In February 2025 he said he would sign and supported House Bill 1, a Republican-sponsored bill lowering Kentucky's income tax rate from 4% to 3.5% starting Jan. 1, 2026.
- In April 2026 he said the legislature's budget underfunds Medicaid, and his administration projected a $681 million shortfall.
- In February 2026 he called for ICE agents to withdraw "from every city and every community that they're in." He also said the agency should be reformed and every agent retrained.
- In March 2026 he said Trump had a chance to "justify the war" with Iran and to explain the why and the how.
- In August 2026 he said Kentucky should end its tax incentives for data centers, saying "I don't think data centers need incentives."

### Cory Booker

U.S. senator from New Jersey. Considering.

Booker says he and his wife will judge a possible run by whether they can offer the leadership the country needs. He argues for renewed economic opportunity, universal health coverage and an agenda to curb corruption across all three branches of government.

- In 2025, Booker and Ted Cruz urged major employers to contribute to children’s tax-advantaged investment accounts, which receive a $1,000 federal seed payment.
- Booker cosponsored the 2025 Choose Medicare Act to let individuals and employers buy Medicare coverage while preserving private insurance options.
- In 2025, Booker introduced the HEAL for Immigrant Families Act to remove immigration-based barriers to health coverage, including the five-year Medicaid waiting period and exclusion of undocumented immigrants from ACA marketplaces.
- In 2026, Booker called for a congressional war powers resolution to end the U.S. war in Iran. He opposed additional weapons for that war while supporting Israel’s defense and deterrence capabilities.
- In 2025, Booker cosponsored the AI Civil Rights Act, which would require testing and safeguards against discrimination in algorithms used for decisions affecting people’s rights and livelihoods.

### Gregory Bovino

Former Border Patrol commander. Considering.

Bovino said in June 2026 that he would consider running if the presidency were needed to carry out mass deportations. He describes his goal as restoring national sovereignty and Americans’ quality of life.

- Wrote on X in June 2026 that his only priority is deporting what he called 106 million people in the country illegally.
- His stated focus is deportation. The unaffiliated draft site lists further items, including a permanent immigration hold, but says it is not affiliated with any candidate.

### Pete Buttigieg

Former Transportation Secretary. Considering.

Buttigieg says he has not made a final decision about another presidential run. His broader argument is that improving family finances requires reforming political institutions, and that Democrats should build for a different future rather than promise a return to the past.

- In a July 2026 speech clip reported by Benzinga, he argued that wealthy Americans can pay a lower effective tax rate than some workers.
- At a January 2026 town hall in Wisconsin he opposed Medicaid cuts and efforts against the Affordable Care Act, per The New York Times.
- At a February 2026 New Hampshire town hall he said enforcement is necessary and that a better path to citizenship is also needed. He said the gap between the immigration the economy requires and what the law permits must close.
- JNS reported in August 2025 that he told Politico he would have voted for Sen. Sanders' resolutions to block arms sales to Israel.
- At Notre Dame in January 2026 he said policymakers should ensure AI benefits everyone as evenly as possible.

### Tucker Carlson

Political commentator. Said no.

Carlson said in July 2026 that he had no interest in running for office. He instead proposed a new party opposed to foreign wars and immigration, saying the existing parties offer too little choice on war and finance.

- CNN says he spoke about the Iran war as a liability for Trump and Republicans from its start. He is a commentator and has no votes or legislation on it.
- Has floated creating a third party while saying he will not seek the presidency, and remains publicly supportive of Vance, per CNN.

### Chris Christie

Former governor of New Jersey. Considering.

Christie said in October 2026 that he would consider a third run only if he saw a path to victory. He argues that Republicans must restore character, integrity and truthful leadership after supporting Trump.

- In 2023, Christie proposed extending the 2017 federal tax cuts and retaining a cap on the deduction for state and local taxes.
- In 2013, Christie signed a budget funding Medicaid expansion under the ACA while vetoing a separate bill that would have made the expansion permanent.
- In 2023, Christie proposed sending the National Guard to the southern border to strengthen immigration enforcement.
- In 2023, Christie proposed banning Russian oil imports and expanding U.S. oil production to put pressure on Russia.
- In September 2026, Christie called for regulations requiring AI companies to provide technical solutions to safety risks that satisfy the government.

### Ted Cruz

Senator, Texas. Considering.

Cruz said in August 2026 that he still wanted to be president but had not decided whether to run. He argues that Republicans should defend conservative economic principles and American leadership abroad.

- Voted yes on H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill, on July 1, 2025.
- In 2017 promoted his Consumer Freedom amendment to the Senate health bill, citing an HHS analysis that it would lower costs and raise individual enrollment.
- Voted yes on S. 5, the Laken Riley Act, on Jan. 20, 2025. The bill requires DHS to take into custody non-citizens charged with theft, among other provisions.
- Said on March 1, 2026 that Trump's decision to strike Iran was the single most important decision of his presidency, and said he had advised Trump that the Iranian government had never been weaker.
- Released an AI framework in September 2025 and introduced the SANDBOX Act, which lets AI developers apply to modify or waive federal rules, with the White House science office coordinating reviews.

### Ron DeSantis

Governor of Florida. Considering.

DeSantis said in March 2026 that he was not planning his next political move. His case for governing emphasizes parental control of education, school choice, immigration enforcement and a strong state economy.

- Proposed a constitutional amendment to cut homestead property taxes. Florida lawmakers put a version on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot that raises the $50,000 exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.
- Signed HB 1299 in June 2025, which extends for two years the protections against discrimination based on a person's mRNA vaccination status.
- Backed the Alligator Alcatraz detention site in support of federal deportations. His office said in 2026 the site had fulfilled its mission, detainees had been moved to other federal facilities, and demobilization had begun.
- Said in a SiriusXM interview aired Nov. 2, 2026 that the Iran war was a "big, big drag" on Republican candidates.
- Has said AI companies should not build data centers and pass the power costs to consumers. NBC News reports his view contrasts with the positions of Trump, Vance and Rubio.

### Rahm Emanuel

Former mayor of Chicago. Considering.

Emanuel says difficult times call for forceful leadership and emphasizes middle-class costs and classroom achievement as he considers a run. After the midterms, he said governing should take priority over discussing the next election.

- In 2026, Emanuel proposed a 10% federal tax on online sports betting and prediction markets to finance research in AI, quantum computing, fusion and other technologies.
- In November 2026, Emanuel called for restoring Medicaid and ACA health coverage funding cut under President Trump.
- In 2026, Emanuel endorsed the Dignity Act, combining border enforcement with a pathway to legal status for undocumented immigrants.
- In 2026, Emanuel proposed ending U.S. military subsidies for Israel and having Israel buy American weapons on the same terms as other allies.
- In 2026, Emanuel proposed diverting 20% of new ICE detention-center funding to community colleges to prepare workers for AI disruption.

- In 2019, Gabbard supported Medicare for All while preserving a private-insurance option.

- In 2017, Gabbard introduced the Stop Arming Terrorists Act to prohibit U.S. assistance to al-Qaeda, Jabhat Fateh al-Sham, the Islamic State and countries supporting those organizations.
- In 2026, Gabbard called for humans to retain control over AI and autonomous machines, citing risks in cyber operations and weapons development.

### Ruben Gallego

Senator, Arizona. Considering.

Gallego has kept a presidential run open and says his decision turns largely on what would be best for his three children.

- His October 2026 economic plan would raise the minimum wage to $20 an hour, require double pay for overtime and aim to triple union density by 2035.
- In August 2025 he and other Senate Democrats introduced the Protecting Healthcare And Lowering Costs Act, which would restore Medicaid funding and permanently extend ACA premium tax credits.
- His May 2025 plan would hire more Border Patrol agents, give asylum officers authority to decide cases, raise the asylum standard and limit access during border surges. It would also offer pathways to citizenship for people brought to the U.S. as children and for undocumented spouses of citizens.
- Jewish Insider reported in April 2026 that he opposes U.S. aid for offensive weapons for Israel, citing military operations in Iran and Lebanon. He said he could not imagine stopping defensive weapons to friends.
- The same October 2026 plan proposes a "robot" tax, an excise tax on large AI companies' revenue to fund public service jobs, and human review of automated decisions on hiring and layoffs.

### Dan Greaney

Comedy writer. Filed.

### Nikki Haley

Former South Carolina governor, UN ambassador. Said no.

Haley said in April 2026 that she would not run in the 2028 Republican primary. She argues that the country should confront hostile governments while both parties address debt, entitlement finances and the economy.

- Her 2023 "Freedom Plan" would eliminate the federal gas tax, reduce income tax rates, make small-business tax relief permanent, and end the state and local tax deduction. This dates from her 2024 campaign.
- NBC News reported in 2023 that she opposed expanding Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act and defended the 2011 Paul Ryan plan to change Medicare to premium support.
- Said during her 2024 campaign that she would add 25,000 Border Patrol and ICE agents, using federal money allocated to the IRS.
- Said in June 2026 that Trump should press Russia and China to stop aiding Iran, including by threatening them with force if the support continues.
- Said in April 2026 that Trump should press China harder to stop supporting Iran and should skip a planned meeting with Xi if the support continued.

### Kamala Harris

Former Vice President. Considering.

Harris says she is still deciding whether to run again, but feels responsible for shaping the Democratic Party's direction and elevating voices and issues she knows through experience.

- In excerpts of an April 25, 2026 speech shared by her team, she called for a "tax code that rewards hard work — not just vast wealth."
- From her 2024 campaign, as listed by TIME on Oct. 10, 2024: $35 insulin for all Americans and a new Medicare benefit to help pay for home health aides.
- From her 2024 campaign, as listed by TIME on Oct. 10, 2024: she backed a bipartisan bill to hire 1,500 border agents and change the asylum process, and she opposed mass deportation.

### Josh Hawley

Senator, Missouri. Said no.

Hawley said in September 2026 that he had no intention of running for president in 2028. He argues that Republicans should protect workers and families from corporate power, technology companies and the movement of jobs overseas.

- Introduced the American Worker Rebate Act in July 2025, which would send tariff rebate checks of at least $600 per adult and dependent child.
- Introduced the Rural Hospital Emergency Room Guarantee Act in June 2026, a 10-year funding stream with a $1,000,000 yearly baseline for eligible rural hospitals' emergency rooms.
- Introduced the Empowering States to Deport Illegal Immigrants Act in September 2022, which would authorize states to enforce federal immigration law.
- Voted no on H. Con. Res. 86 on June 23, 2026, which directed the president to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran.
- Introduced the AI Agent Accountability Act with Chris Murphy in October 2026, which would make operators and developers of AI agents liable for hacking damage in defined cases.

### Chris Van Hollen

U.S. senator from Maryland. Considering.

Van Hollen says he is considering a run because Washington needs major change and Congress alone has not delivered it. He argues for defending constitutional due process and applying U.S. human rights standards consistently abroad.

- In 2025, Van Hollen opposed the Republican budget resolution and backed an amendment to prevent cuts to school meal programs from financing tax reductions.
- In 2025, Van Hollen called for extending ACA premium tax credits and creating a Medicare for All system.
- In 2025, Van Hollen urged the administration to reverse expanded social-media screening for visa applicants, citing free speech and due process protections.
- In 2026, Van Hollen introduced a resolution requiring a State Department report on human-rights abuses in the West Bank, including violence against American citizens.
- In 2025, Van Hollen requested a Government Accountability Office investigation of how the Homeland Security and Justice departments use AI to label people as security threats.

### Brian Kemp

### John Kennedy

U.S. senator from Louisiana. Considering.

Kennedy said in October 2026 that he would consider running if he could no longer be effective in the Senate or believed he could do a better job than the presidential field. He said his immediate plan was Senate reelection.

- Kennedy backed the 2025 tax law extending the 2017 tax cuts and adding tax deductions for qualifying tips and overtime pay.

- In October 2026, Kennedy called for ending the war with Iran, arguing that the United States had achieved its military objectives against Iran’s nuclear program.
- In 2026, Kennedy introduced the AI Emergency Button Act to require human-controlled shutdown mechanisms in AI systems.

### Ro Khanna

Representative, California. Considering.

Khanna says he is weighing whether his economic vision meets the moment: making the future economy work for ordinary families, rather than only AI billionaires.

- In March 2026 he and Sen. Bernie Sanders introduced the Make Billionaires Pay Their Fair Share Act, a 5% annual wealth tax on billionaires. It would fund $3,000 first-year payments to people in households earning $150,000 or less.

- In a February 2026 op-ed he set out seven AI principles, including worker bargaining, a federal AI regulator and third-party verification of advanced models. He also proposed public-service jobs funded by a wealth tax and a tax on AI that displaces labor.

### Wes Moore

### Chris Murphy

U.S. senator from Connecticut. Hasn't said.

Murphy said in November 2025 that he was not planning a presidential run. His broader argument is for stronger unions, higher wages and less concentrated economic power.

- Murphy offered a 2025 budget amendment calling for legislation that would not increase tax breaks for the wealthy. The Senate rejected it by voice vote.
- In 2025, Murphy reintroduced the Choose Medicare Act, allowing individuals and employers to buy Medicare coverage alongside private insurance options.
- In 2025, Murphy introduced the Restoring Access to Detainees Act to ensure people detained by the Department of Homeland Security can contact lawyers and family members.

- In 2026, Murphy and Josh Hawley proposed civil and criminal liability for knowingly operating AI agents that recklessly cause hacking damage. Their bill would also penalize developers who fail to install reasonable safeguards despite knowing, or having reason to know, their agents’ hacking capabilities.

### Gavin Newsom

Governor of California. Considering.

Newsom is weighing a run but says he would not run against Kamala Harris. His broader case is to defend reproductive rights, gun safety and climate action while building an economy based on innovation.

- In June 2026, Newsom proposed taxing asset-backed borrowing, revising inheritance taxes and changing corporate and offshore tax provisions as an alternative to California’s Billionaire Tax Act, which he opposes.
- His May 2026 budget revision proposed cutting $1.8 billion in general fund spending, mainly from Medi-Cal. It included a $20 monthly premium increase for undocumented immigrant adults and reinstated asset tests.
- In September 2025 he signed SB 627, which bars law enforcement from wearing masks on duty with exceptions. He also signed bills requiring warrants for ICE access to schools and health care facilities.
- On March 3, 2026, discussing whether to reconsider U.S. military support for Israel, he said "the current leadership in Israel is walking us down that path." In October 2025 he had said he was not prepared to back a blanket exemption for that support.
- In September 2025 he signed SB 53, which requires large frontier AI developers to publish safety frameworks and creates a channel for reporting critical safety incidents. It also protects whistleblowers.

### Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez

Representative, New York. Considering.

### JB Pritzker

### Josh Shapiro

Governor of Pennsylvania. Hasn't said.

### JD Vance

Vice President. Considering.

In October 2026, Vance said he would consider a run seriously after the midterms, in consultation with his wife, pastor and faith. He says public service should advance the common good, protect vulnerable people and improve access to good jobs.

- Cast the tie-breaking vote on July 1, 2025 for the One Big Beautiful Bill, the tax and spending bill that passed the Senate 51-50.

- At an October 2025 Turning Point event he called for less legal immigration, saying "We have to get the overall numbers way, way down."
- Wrote after the 2026 midterms that "The reason we lost is the war", meaning the war with Iran, and called a recommendation for a diplomatic resolution "sound." He led U.S. negotiators in talks with Iran in spring 2026.
- At the Paris AI Action Summit in February 2025 he said excessive regulation of AI could kill a transformative industry, and said the administration would keep a pro-worker growth path for AI.
